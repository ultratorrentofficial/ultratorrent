Scheduler instructions appear on the torrent itself, through the existing Context-Aware Management Actions framework. The overrides page can only ask for a 40-character info-hash, which is honest and unusable — nobody types one. The four instructions now sit on the torrent, which is the only place an operator forms the intent: they are looking at the thing they want protected. Registered through the existing CAMA registry rather than a parallel mechanism, contributed from the scheduler module's own onModuleInit exactly as the torrent and job actions are, so the registry still knows nothing about the scheduler. Three deliberate choices. They are grouped under maintenance rather than a new scheduler group: the group union is fixed platform-wide by design, because someone who learns where a kind of work lives must find it in the same place everywhere, and widening it for one feature would trade that consistency for a label — they sit beside Recheck, which is the same sort of manage-this-torrent's-participation work. They HIDE rather than disable when unavailable, unlike the lifecycle bar where a disappearing button would move muscle-memory controls under the cursor; these are occasional, and an operator without the permission should not be shown a menu of things they cannot do. And they deliberately do not duplicate pause and resume, which already exist and mean do this now — these mean and keep meaning it, which is a different request. Clearing revokes each kind explicitly rather than through a bulk endpoint, so an undo cannot quietly remove an instruction the caller did not know about. Full en-US and es-PR labels.
