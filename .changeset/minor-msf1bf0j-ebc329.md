Torrent Activity Scheduler, phase 7: bandwidth. The provider contract gains global rate limits, which is the first extension made to it in this whole feature and was justified by inspection rather than assumed. Both shipped engines always supported global limits and already REPORTED them — getGlobalStats reads dl_rate_limit on qBittorrent and throttle.global_down.max_rate on rTorrent — so only the setter was missing from this application's interface. Extending it exposed a capability that existed rather than inventing one, and the methods are OPTIONAL so an engine written by someone else keeps compiling and a caller that finds them absent knows the capability is genuinely unavailable rather than merely unimplemented here. Two conversions live at the adapter boundary and stop there: operators think in kbps and engines take bytes per second, and unlimited is null in the interface but 0 in both engines. Limits are applied BEFORE any pause or resume, because a torrent resumed into an uncapped engine transfers at full speed for however long the rest of the plan takes. The reserve percentages are the honest casualty. A download/seed split cannot be expressed with a single global upload ceiling — the engines offer one number, not two — so a policy asking for one has its overall cap applied and the split reported as unsupported. The obvious approximation, lowering the global ceiling to protect seeding, would throttle downloads in order to reserve bandwidth for uploads, which is the opposite of what the operator asked for; refusing visibly is better than doing the reverse of the request quietly. The reserve fields are therefore absent from the policy editor with a sentence saying why, while the two rate ceilings are writable and enforced. 9 unit tests.
